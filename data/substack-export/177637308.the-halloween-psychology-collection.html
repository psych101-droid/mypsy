🎃 Happy Halloween!

I love this time of year. It’s perfect for exploring the strange, the spooky, and the psychologically intriguing. From Ouija boards and haunted minds to Houdini’s battles with spirit fakers and the real story behind mass hysteria, Halloween invites us to look at fear, belief, and illusion through a psychological lens. In this special collection, you’ll find some of my favorite posts that reveal why eerie feelings exist, why we love to be scared, and what these experiences can teach us about the mind itself. Whether you’re here for chills, curiosity, or a deeper understanding of human psychology, these are the perfect reads for Halloween night.

Why do we seek out fear for fun? This article explores the science of “recreational fear” and why our minds and bodies enjoy it when the threat is safe and controlled. From haunted houses and horror movies to childhood games of peek-a-boo, researchers are uncovering how fear in small doses can boost resilience, emotional regulation, and even stress recovery. A fascinating look at why a good scare can sometimes do us good.

Of course, not all fear is fun. Sometimes, the things that unsettle us arise from what we cannot fully understand or control.

The great Harry Houdini died on Halloween in 1926, the timing of which still fuels rumors of plots and curses. This piece separates fact from folklore, tracing his final illness, his pact with beloved wife Bess to test the afterlife, and the decade of failed séances that followed. You also get Houdini’s own playbook for exposing spirit fakers, from trumpet voices in the dark to slate writing tricks. A sharp look at how a master of illusion spent his life defending truth in a season that loves a good ghost story.

While Houdini fought to reveal the tricks behind the supernatural, others have looked to science to explain why we sometimes believe the unbelievable.

Halloween is the perfect time to ask whether Ouija boards really work, and science has a few answers. Featuring insights from Professor Chris French, this article explores the psychology behind spirit communication, the ideomotor effect, and why our brains can generate movement, meaning, and mystery all on their own. It’s also a fascinating introduction to anomalistic psychology, the science that explains the strange without assuming the supernatural.

Belief can be contagious, especially when fear and suggestion combine, as a famous Halloween broadcast once proved.

When Orson Welles broadcast The War of the Worlds on Halloween eve in 1938, newspapers claimed it sent Americans into mass hysteria. This article revisits what really happened that night, separating media myth from psychological fact. It explores how fear spreads through social contagion, how our biases amplify uncertainty, and how the myth of panic became more powerful than the event itself. A masterclass in how perception, emotion, and suggestion can reshape reality.

But what happens when fear disappears completely, leaving the world calm where terror should be?

Meet S.M., a woman whose amygdala was destroyed by a rare disease, leaving her biologically incapable of fear. Researchers have studied her for decades to understand what happens when one of our most vital emotions goes missing. Her life reveals both the dangers and the strange freedom of living without fear, and what her case teaches us about trauma, survival, and the brain’s hidden alarm systems.

And yet, for the rest of us, not only do we feel fear but also its subtler cousin, the eerie unease that makes our skin crawl.

Why do certain people, places, and moments make our skin crawl? This article unpacks the psychology of creepiness, the ambiguous space between fear and curiosity. From unpredictable social cues to lifelike dolls and humanoid robots, it explains why unreadable minds and mixed signals trigger unease, and how our “creep detectors” can misfire when judging others. A fascinating look at the evolutionary roots of one of our eeriest emotions.

I loved putting this Halloween collection together because what unsettles us often teaches us the most about ourselves. Fear sharpens attention, belief fills in the blanks, and curiosity drives us to explore what lies behind both. Whether it’s a sound in the dark, a strange feeling we can’t explain, or a trick that fools the senses, each reminds us that the mind is its own haunted house, full of locked doors and hidden rooms still waiting to be explored.
