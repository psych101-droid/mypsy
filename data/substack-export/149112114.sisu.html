Sisu is a Finnish word that represents a unique concept, often considered one of the core cultural characteristics of Finland. It refers to extraordinary determination, resilience, courage, and perseverance in the face of adversity or challenging circumstances.

The term sisu doesn’t have an exact English equivalent, but it embodies the idea of having inner strength, guts, and fortitude to keep pushing forward, even when the odds seem insurmountable. It’s a trait that enables individuals to maintain a positive attitude and take action despite overwhelming obstacles.

Sisu is deeply ingrained in Finnish culture and holds historical significance, particularly during challenging times such as wars or harsh weather conditions. It has been seen as one of the key reasons behind the resilience and success of the Finnish people, both as individuals and as a nation.

Sisu holds a profound and almost mystical significance for the Finnish people. Rather than a display of momentary courage, sisu embodies the ability to sustain courage over time. Capturing the essence of Finnish identity and character, sisu epitomizes the philosophy of accomplishing what needs to be done, regardless of the sacrifices involved.

This inherent characteristic of the Finnish people can be likened to attributes like backbone, stamina, guts, and unwavering perseverance. It goes beyond mere hardship, encompassing a sense of integrity that leads to successful outcomes.

Finns demonstrate sisu by bouncing back from failures, learning from them, and displaying unyielding willpower. This indomitable spirit is exemplified in various aspects of Finnish culture: for instance, Finland has Sisu-brand cars and Sisu armored vehicles. Mount Sisu, a mountain conquered by Finnish climber Veikka Gustafsson in the Antarctic, further represents the endurance and determination embodied by sisu.

In an excellent article on the concept of sisu, Dr. Tuomas Tepora states that a person with sisu "calmly but sturdily navigates him or herself in the midst of life’s difficulties, and should these difficulties be overcome, he or she takes the victory without making too much noise about it. But should one fail, things simply go on as usual." According to Dr. Tepora, sisu not only represents heroism under pressure but also composure in the midst of joyful celebrations, making it akin to the famous couplet in Rudyard Kipling’s poem If.

If you can meet with Triumph and Disaster
And treat those two impostors just the same...

To learn about the history of sisu, its fundamental qualities, and whether too much sisu can be harmful, I highly recommend reading the Psychology Today blog post Sisu: The Finnish Secret of Inner Strength and Resilience by personality and cross-cultural psychologist Anu Realo, Ph.D.

Sisu (The Film)

I imagine, like many others, I first came across the concept of sisu following the release of the film of the same name. As enjoyable as it is outrageously violent, here’s the description of the film that accompanied the official trailer:

'During the last desperate days of WWII, a solitary prospector (Jorma Tommila) crosses paths with Nazis on a scorched-earth retreat in northern Finland. When the Nazis steal his gold, they quickly discover they have just tangled with no ordinary miner. While there is no direct translation for the Finnish word sisu, this legendary ex-commando will embody what sisu means: a white-knuckled form of courage and unimaginable determination in the face of overwhelming odds. And no matter what the Nazis throw at him, the one-man death squad will go to outrageous lengths to get his gold back—even if it means killing every last Nazi in his path.'

And here’s one of many great quotes from the film:

'This is not about who is the strongest, this is about not giving up; we have a word for that in Finland.'

As can be seen from the above, there is more to sisu than the white-knuckled grit and determination depicted in the film; however, there’s no doubt that this fascinating concept is now much more widely known.
