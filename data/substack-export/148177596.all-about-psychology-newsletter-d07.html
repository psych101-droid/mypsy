A Detective's History of Psychology

If you’re a fan of detective stories and mystery novels, enjoy piecing together puzzles, or find it fascinating to understand why people behave the way they do, then this book will definitely appeal to you. Can you imagine Miss Marple as a therapist or Sherlock Holmes as a top-notch researcher? If so, you’ve probably already aware of the link between detective fiction and psychology.

This book offers an introduction to important psychological concepts and theories, all viewed through the intriguing world of mystery fiction. It’s crafted for curious minds from all walks of life and delves into the intersection of detective stories and psychology. With engaging activities and questions, this book encourages readers to sharpen their curiosity, enhance their deductive reasoning, and develop critical thinking and psychological insights.

Written by Diane Mello-Goldner Ph.D., a social and personality psychologist and senior lecturer in the department of psychological and brain sciences at Boston University; A Detective's History of Psychology is not only a great general read but also a valuable resource for both teachers and students of psychology.

A Detective's History of Psychology is available to buy from your local bookseller and online internationally from Amazon

It's psychology Jim, but not as we know it!

On the 26th August 1976, American television network CBS broadcast "The Tenth Level," a dramatization based on the infamous Stanley Milgram obedience experiments. The Milgram character (Professor Stephen Turner) was played by William Shatner, A.K.A Star Trek legend Captain James T Kirk and Milgram himself was employed as the show's technical advisor.

You can watch this 1970s classic in full via a playlist on the All About Psychology YouTube channel.
